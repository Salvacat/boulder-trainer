# Movement atlas and performance coaching extension

Research checked 21 October 2026. Personal-use workflow.

## What changed

Eight movement studies connect participant explanations, a three-step practice progression, coach experiments, relevant demonstrations and a local practice log. Six new lessons form performance levels 7–9, building on the gym's third technical course and the vocabulary from course 2. Sixteen new drill adaptations bring the built-in library to 61 drills and the teaching pathway to 18 lessons across nine levels. These levels are skill pathways, not grade bands or qualification standards.

## Evidence and method

Primary coaching explanations and articles were read alongside a 2018 climbing learning/transfer abstract and the full text of a 2024 pilot study. Creator descriptions and chapters were used to assess YouTube relevance; full playback and transcripts were not reviewed. App-authored tasks and progression checks are coaching hypotheses, not validated training interventions.

The 2024 study included 13 advanced female boulderers over five weeks and found no significant difference between the experimental system-wall group and conventional bouldering. We therefore support both repetition and varied task practice, without asserting that either universally outperforms the other. The transfer research concerns specific climbing tasks and cannot establish elite performance transfer or validate our curriculum.

Higher grades do not automatically prescribe smaller holds, heavier loads or bigger jumps. These lessons increase movement choices, linked actions, decision demands and independence while retaining manageable practice terrain. Capacity and coordination can both matter; a missed move alone does not identify the limiting factor. The tool records observations and possible explanations, rather than diagnosing injury or scoring mastery.

## Interface and provenance

The four main destinations remain Sessions, Library, Timer and Coach. Library opens the searchable movement atlas. A topic reveals Understand, Practise, Coach notes and Log panes; Climber view hides Coach notes. Source and advanced material stays behind disclosures. Session picks show the three original curated sessions and personal saved plans; coach lessons have a separate filter. Teaching levels are divided into Core and Performance. The gym's original course outline remains distinct and linked from topics.

## Personal notes and backup

Practice notes are stored under boulder_practice_log_v1 in this browser. A note can capture a person label, observation, possible explanation, experiment, result, next-session check and next action. Notes support edit and undo for the latest removal. Local JSON backups include the notes alongside existing trainer fields. Note imports add missing IDs without overwriting local edits; malformed notes are ignored. No participant notes are added to the optional legacy cloud-sync payload. Clearing browser data can remove local notes, so keep exported copies. Timer backups remain separate.

## Sources and limits

### Opposition vs. Compression

[Climbing Tech Tips](https://climbingtechtips.vhx.tv/videos/climbing-movement-opposition-vs-compression) · Creator explanation

Contrasting directions of contact can stabilise a position; a bicycle combines pushing and hooking.

Limit: A technique explanation, not evidence that one drill improves a particular grade.

### 7 Key Techniques to Master in Climbing

[Movement for Climbers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2LEnGELJxw0) · Video description and chapters

Hip rotation, crossing and flags offer different movement options.

Limit: Options depend on the move and the climber; no single hip position is universally efficient.

### Drop-knee technique

[Movement for Climbers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U0v9xxqEjfY) · Video description

A reference for recognising the drop-knee movement pattern.

Limit: Our entry-and-exit task is an adaptation. Do not force knee rotation or assume a deeper drop is better.

### 4 Lessons to Master Slab Climbing Like Anna Hazelnutt

[Lattice Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUHEnXoWblo) · Video description

High steps and directional pulling are useful aspects of the illustrated slab style.

Limit: An athlete example is not a template for every body. Our tasks do not prescribe maximal crimping.

### Climb Harder With These Momentum Drills!

[Lattice Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K-dTaYG-C1M) · Video description

Momentum can be subtle or explosive, with different timing and coordination demands.

Limit: Our low-move progressions are adaptations; a larger jump is not the measure of learning.

### Side Pulls

[Climbing Tech Tips](https://climbingtechtips.vhx.tv/videos/climbing-movement-side-pulls-1) · Creator explanation

Foot and body position help create a useful lateral pull; gastons are one orientation.

Limit: Use comfortable positions and suitable holds; this is movement coaching, not shoulder rehabilitation.

### A Deep Dive into EVERY Climbing Grip Type

[Lattice Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HBa-ilEp4Os) · Video description and chapters

Grip choice changes with hold geometry and the movement task.

Limit: No grip is injury-proof; this atlas is not a finger-strength prescription.

### Top 10 Projecting Strategies

[Lattice Training / Tom Randall](https://latticetraining.com/blog/bouldering-top-10-projecting-strategies-for-beginners-and-intermediate-climbers) · Coaching article

Projecting includes testing beta, rehearsing unusual positions and starting within the problem.

Limit: Our overlap and finish-link tasks are adaptations; outdoor access methods are not part of these gym drills.

### Kris Hampton: 6 Movement Drills

[TrainingBeta interview with Power Company Climbing](https://www.trainingbeta.com/media/kris-movement/) · Coach interview transcript

Pace, rhythm and tension are distinct practice themes. Slow movement is not always the useful choice.

Limit: An experienced coach’s account, not a controlled comparison of drill effectiveness.

### Climbing technique with the Constraints-Led Approach

[Lattice Training](https://www.latticetraining.com/blog/improve-your-climbing-technique-with-the-constraint-led-approach-cla) · Coaching article

Changing task constraints can invite different solutions and adaptive practice.

Limit: The article advocates one approach. It does not establish that repetition or explicit instruction is always inferior.

### Affordance Realization in Climbing: Learning and Transfer

[Seifert and colleagues, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29892251/) · Primary study · abstract

Manipulated hold orientations were used to investigate exploration and transfer in intermediate climbers.

Limit: A specific research task; it does not validate our lesson sequence or prove transfer to elite performance.

### Development of Specific Motor Skills through System Wall Bouldering Training

[Stien and colleagues, 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2024/5584962) · Primary pilot study · full text

Thirteen advanced female boulderers practised for five weeks. Both groups showed some improvement, with no significant between-group difference.

Limit: Small, specific sample and short intervention. Structured system-wall work was not shown superior to conventional bouldering.
