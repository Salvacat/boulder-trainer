# Coaching research and demonstration coverage

Checked: 21 October 2026.

## Provenance

The original gym course outline remains in initialData.js: Course 1 has four days, with Course 2 and Course 3 topic outlines. Its local grade labels are not V grades. The original user-provided body-tension programme remains separately attributed in docs/body-tension-2026-10-09.md.

The six teaching levels, twelve 60-minute lesson plans, eight rx- drills, observation goals and progression checks are app-authored trainer adaptations. They are not gym-issued material, qualification standards or validated pass/fail tests. Original course outline text is retained. Existing drill instructions were refined to remove forced elbow locking, claims that an open hand prevents injury, blindfolded memory climbing, a fixed-height falling progression, and pressure to execute a feared move immediately. The tennis-ball drill now correctly targets reliance on foot loading rather than claiming to train pinch strength.

## Research method and limits

Direct YouTube links were selected using indexed creator descriptions, chapter lists and official linked coaching pages. Full playback and transcripts were not reviewed. Some videos explain an underlying technique rather than the exact drill. Each in-app link states this relationship and gives a focused observation task. Reusing a source across related drills is intentional. Availability, captions and regional playback can change; videos require an internet connection. Custom trainer-created exercises are outside this built-in coverage.

## Coaching synthesis

Start with the gym topic and a learner goal. Use a short demonstration, manageable practice task, climber self-assessment and one useful feedback point. Adjust the task to the individual, then test it on a new problem and revisit at a later session. These lesson plans specify observation criteria to support the trainer's judgment; levels are not unlocked by grades. Timings are suggestions and include briefing, warm-up, practice, transfer and reflection.

## Primary coaching sources

- [Mountain Training: Foundation Coach handbook](https://www.mountain-training.org/media/g0zntoz4/foundation-coach-candidate-handbook.pdf) — Reflective coaching and varied practice.
- [Mountain Training: Development Coach handbook](https://www.mountain-training.org/media/flxjepna/development-coach-candidate-handbook.pdf) — Adapt practice and feedback to the individual learner.
- [Mountain Training: Bouldering: session delivery and supervision](https://www.mountain-training.org/media/sv4hbbvm/bouldering_guidance_on_session_delivery_and_supervision.pdf) — Choose games and drills for a clear learning outcome.
- [British Mountaineering Council: Get to grips with route reading](https://www.thebmc.co.uk/en/get-to-grips-with-route-reading) — Predict, test and revise a sequence that suits the climber.
- [Lattice Training: Struggling with BIG moves?](https://www.latticetraining.com/blog/struggling-with-big-moves) — Connect movement practice with regular reassessment and feedback.

## Level mapping

- Level 1: Feet, balance & confidence — Technique Course 1 · days 1–2
- Level 2: Read, move & recover — Technique Course 1 · days 3–4
- Level 3: Choose your body position — Technique Course 2 · grips, flagging & crossovers
- Level 4: Connect hands & feet — Technique Course 2 · heel and toe hooks; your body-tension session
- Level 5: Timing, friction & pressing — Technique Course 3 · special techniques and coordination
- Level 6: Diagnose, adapt & transfer — Technique Course 3 · weakness analysis and independent performance
